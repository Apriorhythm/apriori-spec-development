# Delta — protocol (commitment-carry)

## MODIFIED Requirements

### Requirement: the runbook states the per-finding disposition rule
Both runbook editions SHALL carry, in §4 Review & Deliver, a disposition rule for review findings: scope is judged per finding, never by volume; each substantive finding gets a disposition with its basis (current contract / existing constraint / new ask); the necessary-fix criterion — left alone, the delivered product would violate a locatable accepted acceptance condition, a valid owner decision or an applicable existing constraint, cited with the evidence of the violation; a necessary fix is never moved out for its size (split the implementation, keep the dependency); changing a commitment is the owner's decision and its blocking stands until recorded; a new ask the delivery does not depend on is registered as a follow-up with the stable landing grammar `- <ID>: follow-up → <new-change-name> — <text>`, reported as a note and never closed by being moved out; `fixed` / `contract` / `follow-up` / `invalid` / `duplicate` are recommended words, not a closed vocabulary, and an unresolved finding keeps its id, impact and next action; `approach:` in `review-progress` is not a second ledger. §5's rule on what blocks SHALL distinguish a pending item from a registered follow-up in both editions, and the CLI reference SHALL say a registered follow-up is a note; the landing spot SHALL be a valid change name other than the change itself, and the runbook SHALL say that a later authorized session opening that change carries the original id and text as a pending `## Open` item there (an `observed:` line naming the bundle it came from) that only its own exit evidence closes. The P3 prompt is NOT changed (RIB-10 pins it verbatim by a recorded human ruling): the disposition is the producer's duty. Evidence: plan/01 S6 (review-driven scope creep with no gate, ≥5 cases across 5.1/5.2) and the Claude × Astra plan consensus (序 3), which rejected a closed vocabulary and a C8 marker check in favour of this minimal protocol.

## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: a commitment handed across a split keeps its id until exit evidence closes it
Both runbook editions SHALL say, in §4 Specify's Split first and in P2, that a commitment a split hands to another change keeps its id and cannot disappear: if the current delivery depends on it, it stays a pending `## Open` item naming the change that carries it (`- <ID>: <text> — carried by <change>`), blocking until that change's exit evidence lands or the owner accepts it; if the current delivery does not depend on it, it is a follow-up; never both for the same id; and the carried line never keeps the follow-up prefix — turning a registered follow-up into a dependency means rewriting the line without `follow-up →`, since a carry pointer appended to a follow-up line leaves it a non-blocking follow-up. §5 (both editions) SHALL say that a commitment carried into another change keeps its original id there as a pending item until that change's own exit evidence closes it, and that picking it up, archiving the change it came from, or renaming either change never closes it. No new grammar: a carried line without the follow-up prefix is an ordinary pending item to gate C9 / archive R5, while the follow-up grammar stays the only non-blocking form. Evidence: in 5.9 the seven commitments the split notes handed to the end-to-end change were not delivered when it archived, and nothing stopped it (2026-09-26 diagnosis).

#### Scenario: PR-57 Split first and P2 keep a handed-over commitment's id (both editions)
- WHEN §4 Specify's Split first and P2 of `RUNBOOK.md` and `RUNBOOK_cn.md` are read, and a state carrying `- <ID>: <text> — carried by <change>` and one carrying the same id as a follow-up are judged by C9
- THEN both editions say a commitment handed across a split keeps its id — pending and carried when this delivery depends on it, a follow-up when it does not, never both — and that the carried line never keeps the follow-up prefix; C9 blocks on the carried form, reports the follow-up form as a note, and still reports as a note a follow-up line with a carry pointer merely appended

#### Scenario: PR-58 the receiving change carries the commitment as pending until its own exit evidence closes it (both editions)
- WHEN §4 Review & Deliver's landing sentence and §5 of both editions are read
- THEN the later change carries the original id and text as a pending `## Open` item with an `observed:` line naming its origin and closes it only on its own exit evidence, and picking it up, archiving the source change or renaming either change never closes it; the old observed-line-only landing form is gone from both editions

## Notes

Why: the follow-up landing added by scope-disposition made a moved-out ask traceable but let the receiving change pick it up as an observed line only, and nothing tied a commitment the split handed to a sibling to that sibling's exit. In 5.9 the seven items the split notes gave the end-to-end change were missing at its archive with nothing to stop it. Carrying the id as a pending item uses the existing C9/R5 predicate; no new grammar (Claude × Astra consensus NI-CONSENSUS §一 B).
