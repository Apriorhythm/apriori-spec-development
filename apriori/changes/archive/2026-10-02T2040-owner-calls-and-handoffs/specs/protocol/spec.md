# Delta — protocol (owner-calls-and-handoffs)

## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: an item marked as the owner's call stays the owner's, and words recorded for the owner are disclosed
§1 R1's owner-decision paragraph SHALL say that an item a review or an outside report marks as the owner's call — a scope, a caliber, a risk to accept — and that no valid owner decision covers yet is not decided by a broad instruction such as "fix everything else": it stays a pending `## Open` item until the owner answers it, and one answer may settle several such items when it names them (their ids, or "all the owner items as recommended"). It SHALL say that when the agent records in `gates:` a decision the owner gave elsewhere, it writes the owner's words and adds a `note:` saying it recorded them on the owner's behalf and where they came from; the note discloses and does not make the owner's anything they did not say (a reframe kind they did not name, the agent's reading of an ambiguous answer), so it says which parts are the agent's. The same paragraph, and §4's archive `--force` sentence, SHALL name the families `--force` can carry as the code derives them: an escalated review family — an `escalate` verdict, a round past its one automatic re-review that no owner release opened or that still revises, or a `revise` after the owner answered the limit round itself with a `reframe` — and only with the owner's decision already recorded.

#### Scenario: PR-64 a broad instruction does not decide an owner item, and recording for the owner is disclosed
- WHEN §1 R1's owner-decision paragraph is read
- THEN it says an item marked as the owner's call with no valid owner decision is not decided by a broad instruction and stays a pending `## Open` item until the owner answers; that one answer may settle several when it names them; that a decision recorded on the owner's behalf carries a `note:` saying so and where the words came from, and says which parts are the agent's; and that the recorded decision plus `--force` applies to an escalated review family named by its three sources (an `escalate` verdict, a round past the one automatic re-review that no owner release opened or that still revises, a `revise` after the owner took the limit round with a `reframe`), in R1 and in §4's archive sentence alike — neither speaks of a family "stopped at its limit" or keys `--force` on a round's position alone

### Requirement: a delivery dependency handed outside this workflow keeps its id until evidence or the owner closes it
§4 Specify's Split first SHALL say that the commitment rule also holds for something the delivery depends on that is handed outside this workflow — a manual release step, another repository, a person: it keeps its id as a pending `## Open` item naming who or what carries it (`- <ID>: <text> — carried by <step, repository or person>`) and what is still unverified, and only their evidence or the owner's acceptance closes it; a document describing what they must do is not the work done, and a `decision` line never stands in for it. P2's Specify part SHALL say the same in one sentence. There is no new grammar: to C9 and R5 the item is an ordinary pending item.

#### Scenario: PR-65 a dependency handed outside the workflow stays pending, and a document does not discharge it
- WHEN §4 Specify's Split first and P2 are read, and a state carrying `- TS-1: the scheduled-trigger code in task-scheduler — carried by the release step` is judged before and after the owner records `evidence-accept TS-1`
- THEN the runbook says a dependency handed outside the workflow keeps its id as a pending item naming its carrier and what is unverified, that a document describing the work is not the work done and a `decision` line never stands in for it; C9 blocks on TS-1 until the owner's acceptance names it

### Requirement: every /goal recipe names the human stop as a second terminal outcome
Each `/goal` recipe in `docs/operator.md` — Specify, Build & Test, Review & Deliver, and Build → Review → Archive — SHALL carry in its goal condition itself a second outcome: the agent stopped at a human stop (§1 R1; for every recipe that runs Build & Test — Build & Test and Build → Review → Archive — also the 25-turn bound) and its last message names the stop, what is still open and the decision the owner must make — an outcome that hands the change back and is never a pass or a delivery. `docs/operator_cn.md` SHALL carry the same recipes verbatim. The concepts handbook (both languages) SHALL say why: the evaluator judges only the condition it was given, so a stop listed only in the goal's body keeps the loop answering "not yet met".

#### Scenario: PR-66 each recipe's goal condition carries the human-stop outcome, and the handbook says why
- WHEN the four recipes in `docs/operator.md` and `docs/operator_cn.md` and §4.7 of `docs/concepts.md` and `docs/concepts_cn.md` are read
- THEN each recipe's goal sentence carries "OR you stopped at" a human stop with the last message naming it, what is still open and the decision the owner must make, and says that outcome hands the change back and is never a pass or a delivery; every recipe bounded at 25 turns names that bound in its condition; the Chinese operator guide carries the identical recipe text; and both concepts editions explain that a stop listed only in the body does not end the loop

## Notes

Why: the 2026-10-02 analysis of a real project (requirement 5.9, run on v6-dev@59e5c15) found three gaps the Claude × Astra consensus (R59-CONSENSUS §三, batch 1, approved for execution by the owner on 2026-10-02) turned into rules. (1) An outside review listed three items "for the owner to decide"; the owner said "fix all the others", and the agent picked an option for those three itself. (2) A scheduled-trigger handler in another repository was recorded as a producer `decision` ("deliver it as a document"), the `## Open` section stayed empty, and the missing code surfaced only on delivery day. (3) Under `/goal` the evaluator re-prompted twenty times at two human stops, because the stops were listed in the goal's body, not in its condition (the evaluator judges only the condition — code.claude.com/docs/en/goal). The same real run showed a good practice the rule now asks for: the agent added a `note:` disclosing a `reframe` it wrote on the owner's behalf. While editing R1, the paragraph's stale "a review family stopped at its limit" is corrected to what the code checks since limit-ruling. These are written rules; the CLI does not check the content of a note or what an owner item is.
