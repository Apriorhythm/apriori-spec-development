# Delta — protocol (check-recipe-revision)

## MODIFIED Requirements

### Requirement: the operator guide carries an optional requirement-level independent check, landing beside the requirement's sources
`docs/operator.md` SHALL carry, and `docs/operator_cn.md` SHALL carry with the identical recipe block and a Chinese explanation, an optional recipe for checking a requirement end to end: for any requirement, split into several changes or not, before it goes to test, run at the owner's call. The explanation SHALL say that P3 already asks about established behavior, may read callers and re-reviews what a fix affects, and that this check reorganizes the scope and inputs around the whole requirement; and that RUNBOOK §2's risk surfaces can help the owner decide but are not a validated threshold for when to run it. The check is run by a reviewer independent of the session that built the code (a different model or a fresh session, as R2 asks of P3) on the version being checked, with its inputs listed apart from P3's default input: the sources with their versions; each repository's commit checked and comparison base with the whole requirement's diff; the effective contracts at that version — the store as of the comparison base with the deltas of the requirement's changes applied in the order they merge, archived ones included, replacements and removals resolved, a version that cannot be pinned reported as a gap; the owner's recorded decisions; the source checklist. Besides reconciling every `in` checklist row (corrected rows against their ruling), tracing the end-to-end paths across the changes, checking every delivery dependency handed outside the workflow and the assertion and run conditions behind each tick, the reviewer SHALL look at the existing behavior the changes touch and its callers, the other read and write entrances to the affected data for their permission and data scope within the changes' reach, and concurrency, transactions and side-effect consistency on the affected paths; a finding counts only once verified against the code, a refuted one listed with its reason; it reads the code without fixing it. The report, every re-check and the one current checklist SHALL land in the requirement's document directory — never in an archived bundle and never in a change's `review/` — and the report SHALL pin each repository's commit, the source versions and the scope. Findings follow §4's disposition: a necessary fix lands in the change it belongs to while that change is in flight, or in a new change once it is archived, each fix change registering the report in Ground, carrying the finding's id and passing its own P3, then a re-check of the fixes by the requirement-level reviewer written back to the requirement directory, the two closing separately, an earlier check's conclusions never P3's default input; a follow-up with its landing spot; an owner call pending. A clean check opens no change and keeps its report and checklist. The recipe's goal is complete only when the agreed scope is checked, every necessary fix and its re-check are closed, every follow-up has its landing spot, nothing blocking is open and the owner has the report; otherwise the agent continues within its delegation, and only at a human stop, an actual capability block or a set execution bound hands the requirement back, never as a delivery. The owner acceptance or integration script is no longer part of this recipe: it stands on its own (the requirement below). No machine check is added.

#### Scenario: PR-67 both operator editions carry the requirement-level check, its inputs, its dimensions, its landing place and its completion condition
- WHEN `docs/operator.md` and `docs/operator_cn.md` are read
- THEN each carries the explanation (any requirement, split or not, before test, at the owner's call; P3's existing reach stated and the check's increment named as reorganizing scope and inputs; §2's risk surfaces as help, not a validated threshold; independent reviewer on the version checked; inputs apart from P3's default input, including each repository's commit and comparison base, the requirement's diff and the effective contracts — the store as of the comparison base with the changes' deltas applied in merge order, archived ones included; the three added dimensions — regression of touched behavior and callers, permission and data scope of the other entrances to the affected data, concurrency, transactions and side-effect consistency; findings counted only once verified against the code; the report, re-checks and the one current checklist in the requirement's document directory, never in an archived bundle or a change's `review/`; each repository's commit, source versions and scope pinned; fixes back into an in-flight change or into a new change once archived, each with its own P3 and a separate re-check; a clean check opening no change) and no longer the split-only trigger, the "after the last change is archived" timing or the acceptance walk — in both editions; and the identical fifth recipe block carries the commit and comparison base, the effective contracts on the same definition, the three dimensions, verification before a finding counts and the in-flight fix route, and its goal is complete only with the scope checked, fixes and re-checks closed, follow-ups landed, nothing blocking open and the owner reported to — otherwise continuing within the delegation and handing the requirement back only at a human stop, a capability block or an execution bound

### Requirement: the guide keeps a requirement-level check's checklist beside the requirement's sources
`guides/prototype-walk.md` SHALL say that a requirement-level check, run before the requirement goes to test on the version it checks — whether or not every change is archived yet — keeps its successor checklist in the requirement's document directory — not in any change — and that from the start of the check it is the one current checklist: it carries every row over with its id, sources and evidence references and names the checklist and version it continues; a change still in flight references it instead of keeping its own progress, the changes that carry its fixes reference it and never copy it, and carrying a row over does not make its ruling a valid decision or its evidence a verified result; and that a later reconciliation fills `implementation` and `verification` there.

#### Scenario: PR-68 the guide places a requirement-level check's successor in the requirement's directory, before test
- WHEN §5 and §9 of `guides/prototype-walk.md` are read
- THEN §9 names the requirement-level check, run before test on the version it checks, as the one exception to where a successor lives — the requirement's document directory, the one current checklist from the start of the check, carrying ids, sources and evidence references and naming what it continues, referenced by in-flight changes and never copied by the fix changes, carried rows gaining no authority — and no longer says the check runs after the last change is archived; and §5's reconciliation rule points there for a requirement-level check

## ADDED Requirements

### Requirement: the operator guide carries an owner acceptance or integration script that stands on its own and is run before it is handed over
`docs/operator.md` and `docs/operator_cn.md` SHALL carry, right after the requirement-level check's recipe block, a paragraph on an optional owner acceptance or integration script that can be delivered on its own, with or without a requirement-level check: a script for the owner or whoever they hand it to (front end, test), derived from the applicable requirement sources, contracts and any existing checklist, with visible expectations — on the page, or in an API's response when only the back end is the team's; a kit (seed data, commands, a guard before any real side effect, cleanup); real side effects only with their one-shot authorization; problems recorded first and classified after. Before handing it over the agent runs the steps it is already authorized and able to run and says which path it actually ran and what it did not; a mismatch between the script and the code is judged by the contract, a valid decision or the source, not by what the code happens to do. An API run does not stand in for a browser step, and handing the script to someone else still needs the owner's authorization. No gate and no machine check are added.

#### Scenario: PR-69 the owner acceptance or integration script stands on its own and is run before it is handed over
- WHEN the paragraph after the requirement-level check's recipe block is read in both operator editions
- THEN each edition says the script is optional and can be delivered with or without a requirement-level check, for the owner or whoever they hand it to, derived from the applicable sources, contracts and any existing checklist, with visible expectations on the page or in an API response; it keeps the kit, the guard, one-shot authorization and record-then-classify; it says the agent runs the steps it is already authorized and able to run before handing it over and states which path ran and which did not, that a mismatch is judged by the contract, a valid decision or the source rather than by the code's behavior, that an API run does not stand in for a browser step and that handing it on still needs authorization; and it introduces no gate

### Requirement: the runbook says lasting provenance goes in the requirement body, never in the preamble or Notes
`RUNBOOK.md` §4 "Delta grammar and archive" SHALL say, beside its `## Notes` sentence, that a delta file's preamble and `## Notes` never reach the merged spec, so the source, the open status and the basis of a decision that a reader needs to understand the lasting contract go in the requirement's own body, and that an item once pending that now has a valid decision says how it was settled, so an old "to be confirmed" never reads as still blocking. No label, field, checker or merge semantics are added.

#### Scenario: PR-70 the runbook says lasting provenance goes in the requirement body, never in the preamble or Notes
- WHEN §4 "Delta grammar and archive" of `RUNBOOK.md` is read, and a delta with a preamble, a requirement whose body carries a source line, and a `## Notes` section is parsed
- THEN the runbook carries the sentence beside its `## Notes` sentence; and the parsed requirement keeps its source line while neither the preamble nor the Notes text reaches the merged requirements

### Requirement: the concepts guide promises no reviewer tier for semantic faithfulness
`docs/concepts.md` and `docs/concepts_cn.md` SHALL NOT say that a cheaper model tier is enough for the Review & Deliver faithfulness review; the suggested reviewer SHALL be an independent one as R2 asks (a different model or a fresh session), stating that no evidence so far shows which tier is enough — without replacing it by a rule that the reviewer must be at least as strong as the producer.

#### Scenario: PR-71 the concepts guide promises no reviewer tier for semantic faithfulness
- WHEN the model-tier table of `docs/concepts.md` and `docs/concepts_cn.md` is read
- THEN neither says a fast and cheap tier is enough for the faithfulness review, and each names an independent reviewer per R2 with no evidence yet on which tier is enough
